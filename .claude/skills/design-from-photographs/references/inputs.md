# What makes a workable photo set

This method is unusually dependent on its input, because the input is the entire corpus. Judge the set early and say what you find. A weak set does not produce a weak system quietly — it produces a system that looks fine until someone asks where a value came from.

## The one rule that cannot bend

**The photographs must be ones the user took.**

Saved images, mood boards, Pinterest boards and search results are the training data arriving through a side door. Whatever is in them has already been filtered by what the internet rewards, which is the consensus aesthetic. Extract from that and you get the generic result the method exists to avoid, having spent a great deal of effort to arrive there.

If the folder is mixed, ask which frames are theirs and work only from those. If none are, the honest move is to say the method does not apply, and that the fastest fix is a walk with a phone.

## Range matters more than count

Roughly thirty frames with genuine variety is comfortable. Ten varied frames can carry a small system; a hundred frames of one façade cannot carry anything, because they are one observation repeated.

Look for variety along these axes and name any that are missing:

- **Distance.** Wide shots give composition and rhythm; close shots give surface, joinery and material. A set of only wide shots has no texture in it, and no amount of processing will invent one.
- **Subject.** Buildings alone give geometry but no typography, no color events, no small objects. Signage, print, interiors, ground surfaces and everyday objects each contribute something structurally different.
- **Light.** All-daylight sets cannot support a dark mode honestly. If the user wants one, that is a reason to go out at dusk rather than a reason to invent one.
- **Intent.** Frames taken because something was interesting carry more signal than frames taken to document a place. The user usually knows which is which.

## Failure modes worth naming early

**Everything is one subject.** Sixty frames of doors produces a door system. That may be exactly right for a narrow brief and useless for a broad one. Match the scope to the evidence and say which you are doing.

**Everything is contemporary branded design.** Photographing shopfronts of chain stores, or a set dominated by one studio's work, means extracting someone's existing brand rather than the world. Vernacular and environmental design — a hand-lettered sign, a municipal notice, a worn stair — is far richer and carries no such problem.

There is a real line here worth holding: taking *principles* from a designed object is normal design practice, and a menu or a sign is fair evidence about how information gets organised. Reproducing a specific work's layout, mark or type treatment is not. Extract the relationship, never the artefact.

**People are the subject.** Portraits do not yield design language, and publishing frames of identifiable strangers raises a separate problem. Frames where people are incidental — passing through, giving scale — are fine.

**Motion blur and low light throughout.** Structure extraction needs edges. A set that is entirely handheld night shots yields mood but little geometry; say so before promising a component library.

## How to help someone whose set is thin

Be specific rather than discouraging. "Take more photos" is not actionable; these are:

- Name the axis that is missing: "everything here is shot from across the street — a dozen close-ups of joints, edges and surfaces would give the system its texture and material."
- Point at what they already have and ask for its complement: "the signage frames are the strongest thing in this set. More of those, and some interiors for contrast, would carry a full system."
- Tell them what a gap costs: "there is no typography in these frames, so the system will not have an opinion about type. That is fine, but you should know it going in."

The user shooting another twenty frames on purpose beats any amount of processing applied to a set that does not contain the answer.

## Confirm the outcome before starting

"Extract the vibe" can mean a palette, or a palette and icons, or a shippable component library with motion. The gap between the smallest and largest reading is enormous.

Ask directly, and match the promise to the evidence you just assessed. It is far better to say "these frames strongly support a palette, a texture library and about six components, and typography is not in here" than to accept an open brief and quietly pad the thin parts.
